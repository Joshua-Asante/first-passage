# Spec 3: Evaluation and evidence of improvement

**Status:** Draft sprint specification.
**Timing:** Starts in week 1, runs throughout; integrated report in week 6.
**Parent and shared authority:** [Six-week roadmap](../plans/2026-09-18-epistemic-six-week-roadmap.md).
**Companions:** [Foundation](2026-09-18-epistemic-foundation-brief.md); [governed learning](2026-09-18-governed-learning-brief.md).

## 0. Grounding

Use the roadmap's source anchors, especially evaluation-order, lab catalog, question roster and rejection registry. The user reports proposed revisions, failures and repeated prospective evaluations. Use that corpus with its original provenance. Existence does not establish completeness, comparable baselines or improving learning.

## Question and claim boundary

Does evidence-informed revision improve subsequent decisions against a frozen baseline under comparable information, objectives, costs and constraints?

Judge three levels independently. Do not create a weighted score that lets profitability offset constraint violations or documentation offset harmful decisions.

| Level | Candidate measures to select in week 1 | Positive result supports |
|---|---|---|
| Epistemic quality | Support accuracy under a fixed review rubric; appropriate applicability; contradiction detection; repeated-error rate; calibration only when probabilities and resolvable outcomes exist | Better-supported, better-scoped beliefs |
| Decision quality | Predefined opportunity-level utility/loss appropriate to the claim; action/abstention quality versus baseline; avoidable errors; constraint adherence; research cost per useful resolved inquiry | Learning benefits consequential choices |
| Portfolio outcomes | Net performance, drawdown behavior, exposure and achievement of existing portfolio objectives under comparable conditions | Trading value attributable to the revision within evaluated scope |

Traceability completeness is a functional diagnostic, not belief accuracy. Counts of updates, experiments or rejected ideas are not improvement by themselves. Earlier rejection needs evaluation of saved cost and erroneous-rejection risk.

## Historical corpus and baseline

Define the cohort by a recorded inclusion rule/date range before selecting success stories. Enumerate all known proposals in scope: promoted, rejected, failed, abandoned, invalid and awaiting outcomes. Record denominator and unknown/missing entries; retain failures and immature observations.

Capture each original hypothesis, decision target, protocol/criteria, code/data/configuration identities, knowledge/evaluation dates, comparator, outcome, available costs and promotion disposition. Preserve sources and link derived normalization to them.

Classify original prospective evaluation, retrospective reconstruction, exploratory reuse and unavailable/unverifiable evidence. Originally prospective experiments retain that provenance; a new learning rule designed from their outcomes requires fresh evaluation. Never invent a missing historical baseline or prior certainty.

Assess successive revision cohorts while accounting for task difficulty, market context, budgets and unresolved outcomes. If comparability is inadequate, report descriptive findings and case evidence rather than an unsupported trend.

Choose one frozen incumbent workflow. Pair choices on the same opportunity stream and information cutoff, including abstentions and rejected actions. Distinguish a beneficial individual revision from a learning process that repeatedly produces beneficial revisions; the latter needs repeated eligible prospective comparisons including failures.

## Protocol frozen before evaluation outcomes

The week-1 packet must specify:

- Hypothesis, scope, decision unit, protocol owner and version.
- Cohort inclusion rule, incumbent identity and candidate identity.
- One primary decision metric/minimum meaningful effect; supporting epistemic and portfolio measures.
- Mandatory constraint checks and applicable downside/non-inferiority limits.
- Development, untouched chronological evaluation and prospective boundaries; prior exposure to every dataset.
- Minimum evaluable information, horizon, dependence-aware uncertainty method and stopping rule; no arbitrary universal trade count.
- Cost/fill assumptions, opportunity matching, missing/delayed-outcome handling and market/exposure differences.
- Multiple-proposal, repeated-look and reused-data treatment under existing doctrine.
- Research costs and useful failures; explicit improvement, harm/no-improvement, inconclusive and invalid verdict rules.
- Promotion owner and conditions; separate authority for any change to existing gates.

Numeric thresholds are deliberately not selected in this scope brief. Justify and freeze them for the chosen workflow before evaluation, never tune them to observed outcomes. Close defective protocols as invalid/ambiguous; replacements get new identities and fresh eligible evidence.

For rubric-based epistemic judgments, freeze labeled cases and adjudication rules and blind reviewers to candidate identity/outcome where feasible. Record disagreements. An agent scoring its own explanations is not independent evidence of accuracy.

## Attribution and portfolio boundary

Record candidate/baseline choices before prospective outcomes. Compare common opportunities under comparable risk/cost assumptions. Disclose changes in leverage, capital, data or regime instead of attributing them automatically to learning.

Matched simulation/replay produces modeled counterfactuals, not observed alternative fills. State realism limitations. Whole-portfolio changes involving other strategies cannot automatically be attributed to this revision; report measured contribution separately from aggregate performance.

Safety/policy adherence are gates. Decision improvement is the primary empirical target of this build. Epistemic progress can precede financial gains but does not alone establish successful trading improvement. Portfolio outcomes remain essential; insufficient observations leave that claim open.

## Acceptance cases

| Case | Expected result |
|---|---|
| Historical seed | Reproduce an original revision disposition and a failed/rejected disposition, or identify precisely missing evidence; missing evidence remains a delivery gap. |
| Missing denominator | Abandoned/failed proposals are unavailable; process success rates are unidentifiable, though individual cases can still be assessed. |
| Profitable violation | Positive portfolio result breaches a mandatory constraint; reject promotion. |
| Better documentation | Records improve without better choices; do not claim decision improvement. |
| Selected winner | One winner excludes other proposals; refuse a process-level improvement conclusion. |
| Contamination | Selection used evaluation outcomes; classify as exploratory and require fresh eligible evidence. |
| Inconclusive | Uncertainty/incomplete outcomes prevent meeting the frozen criterion; retain baseline and observation plan. |
| Reproducibility | Pinned inputs/protocol reproduce cohort, metrics and verdict within predefined numerical tolerances. |

The week-6 report separately states functional acceptance and epistemic/decision/portfolio evidence. Include identities, coverage, uncertainty, research costs, failures and promotion disposition. Claim verdicts are supported, unsupported or inconclusive; invalid evaluation is separate and cannot support promotion.

## Falsifier and return boundary

If gains disappear under the frozen comparison or depend on omitted failures, later knowledge or unauthorized constraint changes, the claim is unsupported or invalid as appropriate. Do not rescue it by changing the endpoint.

Return protocol, coverage report, reproducible evaluation and claim-specific verdicts to the coordinator. Inconclusive portfolio evidence need not fail the software build, but prevents claiming proven portfolio improvement. Observation beyond week 6 requires a separately scoped follow-on.
