## Answer in brief

1. **The strategy keeps its entry working until the session ends.** The accepted Pine and port place one buy-stop entry per trading day, when the opening range completes. Nothing in either source cancels it by age or places it again. It stays working until it fills or the session-end bar cancels it.
2. **The emulator and the parity evidence use the same lifecycle.** Neither the offline emulator nor the parity harness has an age-based cancel. The ORB port's exact export parity (681 of 681 trades) was obtained under that lifecycle.
3. **The qualification replay uses a different one.** It cancels every resting entry or add one bar after admission (`replay.py:507-509`), as replay-spec RC-9 requires. The ORB port never places its entry again, so in qualification the ORB entry has exactly one bar to fill.
4. **The rail contract says both things.** Rail spec S2 adopts RC-9's one-bar cancel "unless re-issued", while AC-3, AC-8, S4 and the halt/resume §5 cutoff rule each assume entries that rest for many bars. No code on the live path cancels by age.
5. **So the replay meant to qualify ORB does not model the strategy that produced ORB's evidence.** Three lifecycles are candidates (§3). The choice is the operator's.

## 2. The conflicts

**C1 (primary): strategy and parity basis versus the qualification replay.** Rows 1, 2, 3 and 7 keep the entry working from range completion until a fill or the session-end cancel. Rows 4, 5 and 6 cancel it one bar after admission. S2's exception, "unless re-issued", never applies, because the port does not re-issue. The effect is that the qualification replay drops every ORB entry whose level would first be crossed later than the first bar after range completion. That replay would not reproduce the ORB trades in the export the port was accepted against. **How many trades that is was not measured here.** Measuring it needs a run of the private port through the emulator, which this card does not authorize.

**C2: RC-4 versus RC-9, inside the replay spec.** RC-4 fixes the fill model to the parity-validated TradingView semantics (rules 1–8). RC-9 adds an age-based cancel that is not among those rules and that the ORB parity evidence contradicts.

**C3: inside the rail contract.** S2's one-bar cancel conflicts with three other parts of the contract, and S4 assigns the cancel to a component that does not emit it:
- AC-8 has an ORB entry filling at the entry cutoff. The port places its entry only once, at range completion, so that case assumes the entry rested for many bars.
- AC-3 has a resting add surviving until the next session open.
- The halt/resume §5 cutoff rule (row 10) cancels resting risk-adds at the cutoff, which is only needed if entries can rest until then.
- S4 says cancels of stale resting entries come from the adapter, but the accepted port emits none.

**C4: qualified lifecycle versus executed lifecycle.** The live path as built (row 11) follows the port: the entry rests until the scheduled cutoff or the port's session-end cancel, whichever comes first. The qualification replay applies RC-9. Invariant I8 (rail spec `:50`) requires the rail to "execute only the semantics the emulator qualified", including "resting stop entries filled intrabar". The emulator has no age-based cancel. Today the replay that would qualify ORB and the path that would trade it therefore use different entry lifetimes.

**C5: the ORB pre-registration inherits C1 to C3.** ORB-1 refers to "as declared, S2", so the edition's entry lifetime is undefined until the lifecycle is ruled.

**Not part of this question: the scheduled cutoff.** On the rail and in replay, the §5 cutoff cancels resting risk-adds before the Pine's own end-of-day handling, which §5 and RC-8 call an earlier operational overlay. So under any lifecycle that lets the entry rest, its practical end on the rail is the cutoff, not the Pine session end. §5 already records that this overlay changes replay outcomes and requires regenerated ledgers and parity before F1. It applies whichever lifecycle is ruled.

## 3. Candidate lifecycles and their consequences

- **L1 — rest until session end.** This is the Pine-faithful lifecycle. On the rail and in replay the scheduled cutoff and the mode, takeover and incident handling end the entry earlier.
- **L2 — one bar, no re-issue.** This is RC-9 as the qualification replay implements it.
- **L3 — one bar, re-issued each bar.** This is S2's "unless re-issued".

## 4. Questions the operator's ruling must answer

1. **Which lifecycle is ORB's qualified behavior: L1, L2 or L3?**
2. **If L1:**
   - Are RC-9 and rail S2 amended so the one-bar cancel does not apply to ORB's base entry?
   - Is the qualification replay change authorized, given that it changes the E1 freeze inventory?
   - Is it confirmed that on the rail the entry ends at the scheduled cutoff (§5), where that comes before the port's session-end cancel?
3. **If L2:**
   - Is the changed entry rule accepted as a pre-registered behavior change requiring qualification?
   - How is it realized live: a port edition or a rail-side cancel?
   - Which parity reference replaces the exports for ORB?
4. **If L3:**
   - Who re-issues: a port edition or the daemon (see I8)?
   - What happens when a re-issue is refused during the day?
   - How is the cancel and re-placement gap modelled in replay and measured live?
5. **For any choice:**
   - Does ORB-1 state the lifecycle in words?
   - Do AC-3, AC-8 and S4's "stale resting entries" wording change with it?
6. **Before ruling:** does the operator want a count of how many ORB entries in the accepted evidence fill later than the first bar after placement? That count is the size of L2's behavior change. It needs an authorized run of the private port through the emulator, which was not done here.
