## Accounting, failure and timer behavior

The existing START_OWNER controller slot covers one shared-qexec scheduler/launch tail. Use the canonical controller_cpu_guard for the entire active operation: one second thread CPU and ten seconds BOOTTIME, within the already reserved immutable twenty-second orchestration charge and phase ceiling. Historical duplicates cannot enter the active tail or consume another slot. Before reservation, exhaustion or R1/VOID refusal cannot construct runtime, invoke OS lifecycle effects or authenticate originals.

Crash before commit rolls back intent and launches nothing. Crash after commit retains START_INTENT, enrollment, spent START_OWNER and the complete outstanding reservation. Exact duplicate returns historical state without restart. Existing R1 bounded recovery may settle/abort or remain blocked; it cannot reuse START_OWNER, refund uncertainty, or create another draw. Runtime-construction failure after intent follows the same durable recovery path. A new work ID still faces installed phase/no-redraw rules and the same remaining lifetime allowance.

The service must not inherit schedule_campaign_probe's current process-wide arm_boottime_deadline call without scoped retirement. Shared-qexec deadline enforcement must use an owned timer context retired in finally, including failures, and preserve original deadline/boot refusal. No timer from one completed request may kill later work. R4's pre-bootstrap original-deadline issue remains explicitly open; this repair must not claim to solve it through a relative timer.
