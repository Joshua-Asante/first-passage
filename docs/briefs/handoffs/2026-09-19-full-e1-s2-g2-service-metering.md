### 7.2 Design notes

- **D1 — barrier scoping (deviation from a literal reading of §0.5(C), kept to its stated intent).** (C) lists `bind_budget` among the refusing gates and, in the same breath, requires that "the barrier cannot deadlock admission". Those two are incompatible under the guardian's actual order: `campaign_supervisor.py:794` binds the budget *before* `finish_diagnostic_admission` authenticates a pre-admission body at :419-436. An unscoped `_check_budget` barrier would refuse that bind whenever an operator queued a body during the admission run; the guardian would raise, recover itself, mark the admission IN_DOUBT and the body would never be authenticated (validity stays VALID forever). `campaign_supervisor.py` is G1's file, so the resolution is on my side of the interface: the `_check_budget` barrier applies once the campaign is **admitted** (`diagnostic_receipt` retained, i.e. the body is authenticatable on demand through the funded route), while the two grants that create *new work* — `claim_scheduler_bootstrap` and `reserve_work` — refuse a queued body **unconditionally** (that closes the BOUND-but-unreceipted window during the admission run, which is the only pre-receipt path to new authority). Every property in (C) holds: no new positive authority while a body is queued, fail-closed, transient in the fundable case (the same request authenticates immediately under `dispatch_lock`), moot in the unfundable case (terminal/pending; and with every phase ceiling at 120 s, a campaign that cannot fund a 2 s authentication cannot fund anything), never itself sets `validity`, clears only by charged authentication, survives restart. Verified by `test_pre_admission_body_never_blocks_the_admission_guardian_and_is_authenticated_at_the_receipt` and Linux case 2(b). If the coordinator prefers the literal reading, the change is one argument (`admitted_only=False` in `_check_budget`) — but then G1's :794 ordering must move after the authentication, or the admission deadlock above is real.
- **D2 — the charge is an object row folded into the funding projection, not a snapshot field.** See 7.1 item 3. Records: `void_authentication_<seq>` (`qualification_campaign_void_authentication/v1`: attempt_id, sequence, request_sha256 of the queued body, charge_cpu_ns = 2 s, wall_ns = 10 s, trusted clock) and, on a failed verification, `void_refusal_<seq>` (`qualification_campaign_void_refusal/v1`: same identity plus the bounded reason). Both are immutable (INSERT only), like every other `full_campaign_objects` row; `integrity()` validates their binding (sequence contiguity from 1, installed bound, funded profile, receipt present, every refusal paired with the charge whose body digest it names). `_funding_projection` adds the summed charges to `settled_cpu_ns` and subtracts them from `remaining_cpu_ns` with the identical arithmetic the claim applies directly, so `_validate_funding_predecessor` and `_funding_integrity` close over them without any new document version. Tamper cases (charge amount, orphaned refusal, funding total, charge without receipt) are rejected on reopen.
- **D3 — charge before work, settled at claim, never refunded.** The packet's reserve→guard→verify→settle shape collapses to a settled debit at claim time because the settled amount is the full bound whether or not the signature verifies; the "settlement" is the outcome record. A crash between the charge and the outcome leaves a charge with no refusal and no VOID (an *interrupted* attempt): the body stays queued and the exact retry makes one more charged attempt (`test_interrupted_attempt_keeps_the_charge_and_the_body_and_the_retry_is_charged_again`).
- **D4 — refusal reply shapes.** A **refused claim** (terminal, funding/recovery/dispatch pending, insufficient allowance, VOID already, or no funding projection to charge) returns compact `qualification_campaign_status/v2` with `void_pending=true` and the body still queued — the operator sees why nothing verified. A **failed verification** re-raises the verifier's `ValueError` (the transport reply the operator already gets today) after the refusal object and the body clearance have committed. A **queued body on the private route** raises `campaign cancellation pending; new authority unavailable` (an error reply, like the existing pending/recovery/dispatch refusals in `claim_scheduler_bootstrap`), checked before the terminal/state check so a PROVISIONAL or terminal campaign with a queued body still answers with the barrier. `reserve_work` and every `_check_budget` caller raise the same string.
- **D5 — process-local completed-delegation set (gap 4).** G1's `test_service_persists_intent_before_delegating_admission_and_never_resamples_retry` stubs `run_campaign_work` to return status *without* preparing, then forbids any clock sample or launcher entry on the exact retry — which is byte-for-byte the gap-4 state (RESERVED, no intent). Production reaches that state only through a crash (the real `run_campaign_work` prepares or raises). To keep the property that test pins — a retry after a *completed* delegation in this process never resamples — `service.py` keeps a process-local `delegated_admissions` set, added to only when `run_campaign_work` returned normally. It is a no-op in production (the work is then never RESERVED), empty after a restart (so the crash case resumes), and unset when `run_campaign_work` raised (so an in-process failure before the intent also resumes on retry). G1's file is untouched and passes.
- **D6 — restart labels.** `recover_service` skips a structurally absent admission (RESERVED, no transition, no enrollment, no START_OWNER/START_CLIENT/RECOVERY_OWNER slot, no dispatch or recovery row) and reports `ADMISSION_RESUMABLE` when the full resume predicate holds (VALID, PROVISIONAL, no funding/recovery/dispatch pending, same boot, clock not backward, before the admission deadline) or `ADMISSION_UNSTARTED` otherwise (changed boot, deadline passed, VOID, BOUND-without-intent). Both grant nothing. A legacy admission that already carries a RECOVERY_OWNER claim is not structurally absent and keeps today's path (`RECOVERY_PENDING` on a spent slot), see `test_retry_never_launches_unless_the_admission_is_resumable[recovery_pending]`. `inspect_unstarted_admission` answers the common case (started work) from the bounded funding work row and samples the clock only when structurally absent.
- **D7 — non-funded diagnostic release (release/v3, budget profile v2).** No funding projection exists to charge, so post-admission authentication is fail-closed there: the body is queued and status returned; nothing verifies (`test_persistence_only_release_queues_and_never_authenticates`). v3 is the persistence-only install with the scheduler route closed, so nothing runs post-admission on it.
- **D8 — exhaustion is a Windows proof.** The Linux fixture contract budget is 10000 s CPU (`fixture_producer.py:143`), i.e. ~4990 charged attempts to exhaust by 2 s each — not reachable in a bounded run, and nothing on the boundary drains allowance faster than a settled phase. Deterministic exhaustion therefore lives in `test_exhaustion_refuses_the_claim_and_keeps_the_body_pending_without_authentication` (contract budget 1500 s via a test-local monkeypatch of `test_contract._document`, twelve 120 s reservations, exactly twenty charged attempts, then a refused claim with the body pending and the verifier provably not called). Linux case 1 proves the per-attempt charge under the real controller guard and that forgeries never set VOID; Linux case 2(a) proves a refused-claim body staying pending with zero attempts.

### 7.7 Limitations and findings in G1's territory

**Limitations of this branch**
- The §6 bar of twelve green nodes on one recorded run is not met by any of the four runs: my three nodes are green on every run from 35472053638 on (3/3 × 3); the failing nodes are G1's, from the two timing races analysed in 7.6, in code I do not own. Per the coordinator's instruction no fifth run was dispatched.
- Exhaustion by charged attempts is proven on Windows only (7.2 D8).
- A queued body that the funded claim refuses on a BOUND, VALID campaign with `remaining_cpu_ns` < 2 s stays pending and bars new authority until VOID or terminal; no liveness is lost (every phase ceiling is 120 s) but the operator sees `void_pending=true` with no way to clear it except the campaign ending.
- A structurally absent admission that is not resumable (changed boot, expired deadline) is skipped at restart with `ADMISSION_UNSTARTED` and stays PROVISIONAL rather than being terminalised by recovery (the only other path to a terminal state would spend a slot on a never-started work, which §5 forbids).
- `void_pending` semantics widened (7.3); `test_campaign_supervision_linux.py::admit` still passes because it reads only `schema`.
- The VOID→kill wiring (guardian authority poll) is unchanged; a VOID recorded while a probe runs is noticed by `_assert_authority` within one second as before.

**Findings in G1's territory (findings only; nothing changed)**
1. `campaign_supervisor.py:930-939` (`recover_campaign_work`) claims RECOVERY_OWNER before `_recover_campaign_work` sees the RESERVED branch; on `f2606b0` a service restart therefore spends the one-use slot on a never-started admission and a second restart leaves a permanent `RECOVERY_PENDING` barrier. My `recover_service` skip avoids calling it for that case; the entry itself still behaves this way for any other caller.
2. `campaign_supervisor.py:794` binds the budget before `finish_diagnostic_admission` authenticates a pre-admission body — the reason the `_check_budget` barrier is scoped to admitted campaigns (7.2 D1). A forged pre-admission body makes `finish_diagnostic_admission` raise (`campaign_store.py:435`), so the guardian recovers itself; the campaign is left BOUND without a receipt and, with the barrier, without any route to new work — arguably the right outcome, but the refusal is never retained and the body is never cleared.
3. **Guardian self-recovery kills the recoverer before completion.** `LinuxCampaignRuntime.cleanup` (`campaign_supervisor.py:538-546`) SIGKILLs the guardian PID in the work group; when the guardian's own `except BaseException` path (`:807-810`) runs `recover_campaign_work`, that cleanup kills the guardian before `_complete_recovery`, leaving a recovery row with observations and no completion (permanent `recovery_pending` for the attempt) and no traceback in the journal. The exception itself is lost with the traceback.
4. **Probable trigger of 3 for fast-exiting payloads:** `_run_probe` (`:878-880`) reads `/proc/<pid>/cgroup` for the Pid that `docker inspect` just reported as running, outside the `FileNotFoundError` guard that protects the per-process loop below it; a `noop` payload exits ~1 s after start, exactly when both self-kills happened. A `Running=false` inspection with a still-nonzero `Pid` (`:891-892`) is a second candidate. Either is a G1-side fix (guard the read, or treat a vanished Pid as "not running").
5. `test_s2_warm_service_starts_one_guardian_per_work_with_no_scheduler_unit` reads `systemctl show <guardian_unit> Slice` after waiting for COMPLETED; in every run (green baseline included) the guardian unit is deactivated within the same second as its COMPLETED clock (baseline 19:44:50.344 → deactivated 19:44:50; runs 1/2: 21:55:56.5 → :56, 22:15:25.2 → :25), so the assertion races the unit's garbage collection. 6. Only seven of the nine existing S2 cases are manifest nodes; the two R2b route cases run only because the file runs in full (7.1).
7. `campaign_host.restart` stops the unit under a 15 s `subprocess.run` timeout with `check=False`; a SIGSTOPped supervisor would exceed it (SIGTERM is not delivered while stopped) — the harness caller must SIGKILL first, as my case 3 does.
