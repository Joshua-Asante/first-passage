## 1. Decision rules

1. Start with required observable behavior, then seek a vendor owner. Do not start by defending local modules or assigning them permanent ownership.
2. Every retained local responsibility must name its requirement, vendor alternative, exact missing capability or mismatch, and cheapest sufficient implementation boundary. “Safer,” “more control” and “already built” are insufficient reasons.
3. Every vendor allocation must cover the exact Tradovate route and feature combination, normal operation, relevant failure/restart behavior, observability and residual operator work. NinjaTrader support is not Tradovate support; broker-native protection is distinct from CrossTrade-managed work.
4. Keep two explicit columns of conclusions: **preserves accepted behavior** and **simplification requiring a named decision/requalification**. Do not silently change strategy timing, sizing, protection, opportunity counters, takeover, priority or recovery rules. Equally, do not automatically build a complex replacement when a reasonable behavior change should be offered to Joshua.
5. Distinguish decision ownership, order execution, monitoring and recovery. A controller can admit an intent while delegating its subsequent lifecycle. An observer acting after execution cannot enforce pre-execution admission. Every mutation path must respect the chosen boundary, including direct alerts and operator/manual paths under their actual scope.
6. Assess platform permissions separately from technical capability. Current TradingView §3 language raises a non-display/automation restriction; do not infer permission from webhook availability or vendor marketing. Identify any applicable written permission/license evidence or the precise clarification owed. Do not contact vendors. A permission gap conditions adoption but need not stop the technical map.

## 4. Acceptance cases and falsifiers

These are documentary walkthroughs, not permission for broker tests. Trace the recommended path through concrete events and expected outcomes; list the minimum later observation needed where evidence is missing.

| Case | Fails acceptance if the map… |
|---|---|
| Two eligible legs arrive in opposite orders near shared capacity | Depends on network arrival order where portfolio priority is required, or leaves admission after execution. |
| Pine simulates a base; actual entry is refused, partial or unknown; an add follows | Treats emulator state as confirmed exposure, assumes refusal refunds decision counters, or invents feedback into Pine. |
| ORB stop is placed, cancelled or triggered; exit follows | Conflates placement with simulated fill, or changes resting-stop execution to later market entry without a named decision. |
| Striker initial bracket under the §59 edition; Vanguard triggered trail (if live) activates and reverses between messages; ORB's trail is removed by the §59 edition, so do not assume it | Assumes timely protection or reconstructs an intrabar path from final OHLC without a qualified owner. |
| Native protection fills before Pine exits; takeover or scheduled flatten overlaps | Leaves two conflicting owners, residual orders, incorrect leg attribution or an unobserved close. |
| Webhook is duplicated, lost, late or from an obsolete alert snapshot | Assumes exactly-once delivery, confuses receipt with execution, or relies on unsupported broker idempotency. |
| Entry/cancel/amend/close response is lost; vendor work may continue | Treats timeout, flatness, empty history or acknowledgment as proof of no future effect; adopts proposed recovery policy as accepted. |
| Local/vendor restart, source silence or session/contract rollover | Has no named state/input restoration producer, dispatches stale backlog, or requires an absent Pine tick for an independent deadline. |
| Delete a proposed feed, daemon, module or state store | Leaves any retained live/replay consumer or recovery input unsupported, or hides equivalent replacement work. |
| Delegate a capability to a vendor | Uses another destination's feature, unspecified configuration/entitlement, or happy-path evidence to claim failure guarantees. |

Self-review the final matrices for orphan capability IDs, duplicated decision owners, missing producers, unsupported deletions and unjustified local retention. Every deletion maps to surviving capability ownership; every retained controller function maps to a substantiated gap. Contradictions may remain as explicit decision blockers, not buried assumptions.
